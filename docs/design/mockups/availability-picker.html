What this asserts — argue with it

  • Guests → date → time, in that order. Party size comes first because it filters availability: a 15-person party only sees days/times where a boat that big is free. "We fit you to a boat — up to 16" sets the expectation that the customer picks a size, not a hull.
  • The calendar is the virtual model made visible (DEC-125). Each day shows its lowest price ($399 Sun, $549 Sat, $499 weekday — schedule-driven); a day with nothing free is greyed. No rows underlie this — it's schedule × vessels × dates − blocks − bookings, computed. Jul 20–24 greyed = the Brew 3 block plus the rest booked.
  • Times show availability as "N boats free · up to M," not seat counts. M is the biggest boat still open at that time — the customer's real ceiling. "1 boat left · up to 12" nudges without inventing scarcity.
  • Boat is never named or chosen. The customer taps a time; the claim assigns a boat that fits their party at booking. This screen and the booking form agree: offering + party, boat behind the scenes.
  • Price shown up front, per day and per time — no surprise at checkout. Matches the live brewcle.com widget's month-with-prices, trimmed to the whole-boat case.
  • Tapping a time → the booking form, carrying the slot (Sat Jul 18 · 1:30 PM). "Change" on the booking form comes back here.
  • Open: if BrewBoat ever runs more than one offering, does this open on an offering list first? Waitlist on a sold-out time? Show "only 1 left" as urgency or is that too Xola-salesy for the brand?
