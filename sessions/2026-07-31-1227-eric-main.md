**The issue asked for a DEC and there wasn't one to write.** The operator's challenge —
*"is there a dec saying we are using In / Out?"* — was the right question and the answer was no.
Five DECs mention In/Out only descriptively (DEC-030, -041, -089, -117 name "the In/Out screen");
DEC-MSG-3 already writes "inline Yes/No" for its Telegram option. The vocabulary's only home was
SPEC §2.6.1's **example line**. Third time in two sessions that a reach for a DEC didn't survive
contact with the tell from #599 — except this one inverts it: a sentence in SPEC *does* stop being
true, and it's still not a decision, because the sentence is an illustration. **Refinement worth
keeping: the test isn't "does a SPEC sentence change", it's "does a RULE change."**

**A second false claim in the issue, caught by reading the script.** Its AC said `check:decisions`
"fails on an undeclared spec amendment, so this is a real gate." It doesn't —
`scripts/check-decisions.mjs:118-171` validates only DECs that *declare* an amendment (section
exists, scope present, banner regenerated). Nothing scans SPEC for undeclared edits; it can't.
The gate cited as the reason to trust the process ran in the other direction entirely.

**The issue's scope was also short by three surfaces and wrong about one file.** It named four and
listed `src/asks/answer-as.ts` — which has no user-visible copy at all (doc comment only; the real
buttons are in `components/outbox/`). Meanwhile the `/crew` notices ("You're in…", "Marked out…"),
the outbox notice ("You answered: in."), and the audit pills all spoke the old vocabulary and went
unlisted. Reading the source found them; trusting the issue's scope would have shipped two
vocabularies. The operator's own constraint on the pills — *"as long as it's not surfaced to the
UI"* — turned out to be already satisfied: `in`/`out` are **derived** display kinds
(`audit-trail.ts:76-90`), the DB stores `ask_accepted`/`ask_declined`.

**Test-first, and the constant had no test at all.** Flipped the four production assertions in

**Code review caught me falsifying a dated record.** `forward-board-alerts.ts:84-86` narrates the
