**Code review found a silent underpayment, in the one column Gusto pays from.**
`(minutes / 60) * 100` is not `(minutes * 100) / 60`. For 69 minutes — **exactly 1.15 hours** —
the first spelling yields `114.99999999999999`, so flooring emitted `"1.14"`. A whole extra cent
*below* the deliberate §2.9.6 truncation, not an instance of it. Swept: **4,588 of the first
200,000 whole-minute totals**, every one underpaying, and **scattered** (69, 123, 138, 153, 246…)
rather than clustered — so the handful of hand-picked cases I'd written would have missed almost
all of them. **The lesson is about the shape of the test, not the arithmetic:** for a defect whose
instances are sparse and irregular, examples are the wrong instrument and a sweep against exact
rational arithmetic is the right one. Pinned with both, and negative-controlled.

**Playwright genuinely could not test the thing I most wanted tested.** Its env is per-RUN, so no
spec can turn `TIME_CLOCK` off for itself — and #621 is this repo's own example of a kill switch
that hid nav links and left routes live. Rather than accept a one-line read as the only assurance,
the gate is asserted structurally: every file under the three time-clock directories must call the
predicate, and the count of `if (!timeClockEnabled())` must equal the count of exported actions.
It includes a guard against approving of nothing if a directory moves. Removing one action gate
reddens it with `3 actions but 2 gates`.

**Negative controls did the load-bearing work again.** UTC bucketing instead of vessel-local
reddens the 8pm-Eastern tests in *both* directions (hours pushed out AND leaked in); an inner join
instead of the union reddens **eight**; removing the export guard reddens the refusal.

failed on the clean tree — checked by stashing. It adds a punch 09:00–17:00 on the period's FIRST

fourteen, before 5pm. Today was that day. Moved those tests to the previous period — kills the
class rather than patching four call sites. One of them also skipped itself exactly when the
period started today, i.e. the day its assertion mattered most.

**Left open on purpose:** whether 2dp is the right precision at all is Gusto's answer, not ours
