## Task 1: Cancel and refund a booking, and reconcile dashboard refunds (issue #616)

**Completed:**
- `src/reservations/cancel-reservation.ts` + `refund-payment.ts` (new). Cancel writes the
  reservation AND its event, re-forms shifts, relays crew notices. Refund takes one editable
  amount and splits it across charges newest-first.
- `charge.refunded` → `refund_recorded` through the port, both adapters, and the webhook —
  outside the RESERVATIONS gate, since money that already moved must record in any deployment.
- Two new port methods (`cancelEventIfUnclaimed`, `getPaymentByIntentId`), both adapters,
  contract cases. One index migration. **DEC-153** revises DEC-107 (refund mechanism) and
  amends SPEC §3.3 (status claim only).
- 2073 unit, 124 Postgres, 13 e2e × 2 widths, three doc checks, both typechecks, lint, build.

**Cancelling the reservation does not free the boat, and cancelling the event bricks the slot.**
The reservation flip releases only its own slot; `hull-busy` and the overlap guard count every
`scheduled` event, so a cancelled 13:30 keeps 14:00 unsellable, and `formShifts` only collapses a
shift when every event on the vessel-day is cancelled — the crew stay rostered. So the event is
cancelled too. But `events_muster_slot_identity` is status-agnostic, so a cancelled row keeps
owning its identity and `saveBookingIfSlotFree` returns `lost` **forever** — cancelling would
permanently destroy the boat-slot. The claim path now resurrects in place. The Postgres test
failed on `expected 'lost' to be 'booked'` before the fix; `InMemoryRepository` would have hidden
it (resurrect-by-Map-key-collision, right for a deterministic id, wrong for an override row).

**The operator rejected the first refund design and was right.** Per-payment-row presses were me
dodging the allocation problem and pushing it onto him mid-cancellation: *"cancel for so many
reasons and who knows what the refund amount might be."* One editable box now. He then asked why
it splits at all rather than coming off the total — the answer is that there is no total at
Stripe: `refund()` takes a `paymentIntentId` and caps at that charge. The split is the
translation, not a modelling choice.

**Both refund figures render next to their radio option.** With no client JS a single number
cannot follow the selection, and a figure silently belonging to the other choice is worse than
none on a screen whose only job is deciding an amount. This is what `refund-terms.ts` was built
for at #619 and had no caller for.

**Review found four real defects, two of them money-severity.** `parseDollarsToCents` stripped
commas unconditionally, so `"1,50"` was a **100× refund** ($150.00 for $1.50) with the success
copy, the ledger and the CAS all agreeing. The cancel released the hull with an unconditional
`saveEvent` — a read-then-write on a slot every other path locks, so a cancel racing a re-book of
the slot it just freed could cancel a customer who had paid; now a conditional write under the
same hull-day advisory lock, with the locks acquired in the same order (my first cut inverted
them, which deadlocks). A partial refund failure rendered as a green success — "Refunded $0.00 to
the card it came from" when the FIRST leg failed. And the in-memory resurrect kept the dead
booking's price where Postgres nulls it, negative-controlled at `expected 50000 to be undefined`.

**I measured the #718 defect rather than eyeballing it, and it was there.** After cancelling,
"Resend confirmation" landed **8px** into the coordinates "Cancel this booking" had just vacated —
a second tap fires a different action against a booking just cancelled. Destructive block moved
last so collapsing it moves nothing; 71px clear, frozen as an e2e. Written after the fix, but the
failure was observed first.

**I did not amend the spec on my own initiative this time.** Flagged that SPEC §3.3's "nothing in
`src/` implements a cascade or a refund" had become false and asked; he said fix it. Last
session's lesson held.
